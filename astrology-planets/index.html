Astrology's Planets Explained: From Ancient Babylon to Modern Discoveries

Astrology, explained like you are five.

Open any birth chart and you will meet a cast of characters: not cold floating rocks, but cosmic personalities. That is how ancient stargazers saw the planets — gods and archetypes shaping the human mind — and astrology's planet gallery has kept growing ever since, from seven naked-eye wanderers to dwarf planets discovered this century. Here is the full cast, and where each one came from.

Start with the classical seven: the Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn — the bodies visible to the naked eye, the ones Babylonian sky-watchers tracked thousands of years ago. They mattered so much that they built our week: seven bodies, seven days, from Sunday's Sun to Saturday's Saturn. Look at a calendar in English and you are still reading a Babylonian planet list.

In astrological terms, the two luminaries are the core of the personality. The Sun is the radiant, conscious ego — who you are trying to be. The Moon is the deep, nurturing subconscious waters — what you need to feel safe. Around them orbit the personal drives: Mercury, the rapid-fire intellect and communicator; Venus, the artist hunting harmony and romance; Mars, the warrior spark of action and desire. Then the social heavyweights: Jupiter, the expansive planet of luck and growth, and Saturn, the strict taskmaster of discipline and limits — the two that astrologers say set the long arc of a life.

Then telescopes ruined everything — in the best way. From Uranus on, each new discovery arrived with a matching generational story. Uranus, found in telescope age, became the Rebel: sudden upheaval, invention, revolution. Neptune, the Dreamer: oceans, illusion, spirituality. And Pluto, the Transformer: demolition and rebirth, the underground power. Astrologers call them the generational planets — slow movers whose traits color whole eras, not just individuals.

The gallery kept expanding to the very small and very far. Ceres, spotted in 1801, carries the nurturing soil — the asteroid belt's mother archetype. Eris, named in 2005 after the goddess of discord, brings raw truth-telling chaos — the dwarf planet that shook up astronomy itself by forcing Pluto's reclassification. Even tiny rocks get roles in this theater.

Each planet also comes with real estate: a natural ruling zodiac sign and an astrological house, its home address in the chart's twelve rooms. Mars rules Aries, Venus rules Taurus — and where a planet sits in your chart decides which room of your life its character plays in.

And under it all lies the ancient blueprint, the hermetic principle "as above, so below": the sky mirrors the soul, and reading one means reading the other. Whether you see deep psychology or ancient poetry in that mirror, the principle served as humanity's original guide to the inner cosmos — a psychology textbook written in planets, three thousand years before the word existed.

The short version

  • The classical seven naked-eye planets (Sun through Saturn) shaped both astrology and our 7-day week — a Babylonian inheritance from Sunday's Sun to Saturday's Saturn.
  • Personalities stack in layers: Sun/Moon as ego and subconscious, Mercury/Venus/Mars as personal drives, Jupiter/Saturn as life's arc — then telescope-era Uranus (Rebel), Neptune (Dreamer) and Pluto (Transformer) as generational forces.
  • Even Ceres (1801) and Eris (2005) joined the cast, each planet owns a ruling sign and house, and all of it rests on the hermetic "as above, so below".

FAQ

Why do the days of the week match planets?

The week was built from the seven classical, visible bodies: Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn. English still shows it — Sunday (Sun), Monday (Moon), Saturday (Saturn) — a living fossil of Babylonian sky-watching.

What's the difference between personal and generational planets?

Fast-moving classical planets (Mercury, Venus, Mars...) describe individual personality; the slow outer planets (Uranus, Neptune, Pluto) take years per sign, so astrologers read them as traits of whole generations rather than single people.

Why are Uranus, Neptune and Pluto called "disruptors"?

They were discovered by telescope, not naked-eye tradition, and each arrived as a cosmic upstart: Uranus the Rebel of upheaval and invention, Neptune the Dreamer of illusion and spirituality, Pluto the Transformer of destruction and rebirth.

What does "as above, so below" mean?

The hermetic principle that the sky mirrors human life: planetary patterns above correspond to psychological patterns below. It is the founding axiom of astrology — and, as the video notes, arguably humanity's first framework for mapping the mind.
