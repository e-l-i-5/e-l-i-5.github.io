Why You Count to 10 (And How to Count to 12 on One Hand!) ✋🔢 (ELI5)

Why do we count to ten? It feels like the most natural thing in the world — but only because you own the equipment. Imagine counting a giant pile of berries in a world where the numbers 1, 2 and 3 were never invented. Impossible? Not at all. Long before calculators existed, our bodies were the very first counting machines, and different cultures programmed those machines in wildly different ways.

Look at your hands: four different counting codes are hiding in them. The obvious one is base-10, decimal — ten fingers, ten digits, the system most of the world uses. But the Yuki people of California counted the four spaces between fingers, giving them base-8, octal. The ancient Maya used all ten fingers and all ten toes — base-20, vigesimal, the full-body calculator. And some cultures tap the twelve finger joints of one hand with the thumb — base-12, duodecimal, which is the secret origin of our "dozen". One pair of hands, four operating systems.

Some calculators refuse to stop at the hand. In Melanesia, body-counting maps numbers onto the whole body: start at the fingers, climb the wrist, the elbow, the shoulder — a walking calculator where your arm is the number line.

Then comes the strangest trick of all: dog math. On some islands, people used the word "asu" — dog — to mean the number four. Counting "one, two, three, dog" sounds like a joke, but it is a real numeral. And it could fool outsiders: when a farmer asked for "50 dogs of pigs", he did not want 50 pigs. He wanted 50 fours — and walked home with 200 pigs. A number word, wearing a fur coat.

Our own numbers carry fossils too. Why does the clock run on 60 seconds and 60 minutes? That is the Sumerian legacy — their counting system survives every time you check the time. Meanwhile China groups big numbers by ten-thousands (the "wan"), not by thousands, so giant numbers are read in a different rhythm entirely. And when numbers get truly super-giant, English builds names like centillion — a number with a staggering 303 zeros behind it.

So the next time someone calls math "universal", show them your hands. Ten fingers gave us ten digits, but four spaces gave others eight, and twelve joints gave some a dozen. Numbers are not handed down from the sky. They are built from bodies — and every body is a slightly different calculator.

FAQ

Why do humans count in tens?

Because most of us carry the counting tools with us: ten fingers. Body-based counting was humanity's first number system, and base-10 is simply the version that won — not the only one that ever existed.

How do you count in base-12 on your hands?

Use the thumb as a pointer and tap the twelve finger joints of one hand: four fingers, three joints each. That built-in dozen is why we still buy eggs by the dozen — a base-12 survivor in a base-10 world.

What is "dog math"?

On some islands, the word "asu" — dog — meant the number four, so counting ran "one, two, three, dog". A farmer asking for "50 dogs of pigs" wanted 50 fours: 200 pigs. Number words can hide inside ordinary nouns.

Why does the clock use 60 minutes and seconds?

That is the Sumerian legacy, preserved from their counting system into our timekeeping. Every glance at a clock runs on arithmetic older than writing — a body-counting fossil that outlived its inventors.
