The Goblin Ore That Runs Your Phone

Five centuries ago, German miners digging in the ore mountains kept pulling out rock that looked like silver but refused to behave like it. They decided the ore was bewitched and blamed mischievous underground spirits, the kobolds -- and the metal took its name from the goblins ever after. That cursed rock now sits in almost every pocket on the planet. Your phone is powered by goblin ore, and the story of how it got there runs from ancient paint shops to a Congolese mine shaft.

Cobalt's career started long before the curse. Ancient jewelers ground it into a brilliant blue paint that outlasted almost everything around it -- the silvery-blue color that still carries the metal's name. The modern chapter opened in 1991, when Sony used this "goblin ore" to launch the portable tech revolution: a battery chemistry that finally let electronics leave the desk. The phone in your hand is a direct descendant of that moment.

Inside the battery, the video gives cobalt a job title: personal trainer. It keeps your gadgets from getting too hot and helps the battery last longer -- discipline the cell needs to survive daily charging. The video credits the metal with 60% of the market, which is why every gadget maker on Earth cares deeply where their cobalt comes from. The trainer keeps the whole portable world cool, literally.

Then the story turns. One single country -- the Democratic Republic of the Congo -- holds almost all of the planet's cobalt: the video calls it the 75% rule. And the supply chain behind that number has a heartbreaking human cost. The metal is pulled from dangerous tunnels, and the mining involves child labor -- real children doing real harm to their bodies so that pockets elsewhere can stay charged. The goblin curse, it turns out, never left the ore; it just changed victims.

Which is why the future is a race to fire the goblin entirely. Engineers are chasing batteries built without cobalt, and the front-runner already has a name: the LFP battery -- safer, cheaper, and, as the video puts it, goblin-free. The metal that began as an excuse for mischievous spirits, painted the ancient world blue, trained the modern battery, and exposed an ethical wound may end its career the way it began: by refusing to behave like anyone expected.

FAQ

Why is cobalt called a goblin metal?

Sixteenth-century German miners kept finding ore that looked like silver but did not behave like it, so they blamed mischievous underground spirits called kobolds. The name stuck to the metal and survives in the word cobalt itself.

What does cobalt actually do in a phone?

Inside the battery the video casts it as a personal trainer: cobalt keeps the cell from getting too hot and helps it last longer, and the video credits it with 60% of the market. Sony's 1991 use of cobalt chemistry kicked off the portable tech revolution.

Why is cobalt mining an ethical problem?

About 75% of the world's cobalt comes from a single country, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and much of it is mined in dangerous tunnels with child labor involved. That human cost is the main driver behind cobalt-free batteries like LFP.
