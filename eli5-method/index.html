The Secret Weapon of Smart Communicators: How ELI5 Changes Everything

The ELI5 method itself, explained like you are five.

Every expert carries a curse, and it is called the curse of knowledge. Once you deeply understand something, it becomes physically hard to imagine not understanding it. So when an expert explains their field, the jargon pours out — dense, precise, and completely sealed to anyone outside the guild. The audience nods, understands nothing, and walks away. This is the problem ELI5 was invented to break.

ELI5 stands for "Explain Like I'm Five". It began as an internet acronym — a casual request on forums: drop the jargon and explain it as if I were a small child. Nobody planned a revolution. But the idea was too useful to stay slang, and over the years it evolved into an essential communication framework used by top engineers, researchers, and even global security experts. From a joke online to a professional tool — that journey is the first lesson: simplicity scales.

The timeline tells the story. Around 2016–2019, ELI5 moved from internet slang into print: style guides and professional writing started treating "explain it like I'm five" as a legitimate method, not a meme. In 2016, engineering teams used it for scalable storytelling — explaining complex systems so that whole companies, not just specialists, could follow. In 2017, librarians applied it to genealogy and family history, helping ordinary people navigate archives built for scholars. And in 2019, ELI5 reached the most serious room imaginable: global nuclear security, where experts used plain-language frameworks to communicate risk verification across languages, agencies and borders. If it works for nuclear risk, it works for your slide deck.

The core move is a question: "Can you verify?" Can the listener repeat the idea back and check it against their own experience? If not, the explanation has not happened yet — only the words have. ELI5 turns explaining from a speech into a loop: explain, check, adjust.

And it loops again with learning. ELI5 pairs naturally with TIL — "Today I Learned" — the habit of capturing one clear new thing per day. To write a TIL honestly, you must first ELI5 it to yourself. If you cannot explain it simply, you have not learned it; you have only met it. That is the learning loop: simplify to understand, understand to remember.

So here is the secret weapon, laid bare. Simplifying is not dumbing down — it is the opposite. It is proof of mastery, the mark of someone who knows a subject so well they can rebuild it from scratch in front of a five-year-old. The experts who struggle to explain are not too smart; they are simply still cursed. ELI5 is the cure.

FAQ

What does ELI5 actually stand for?

"Explain Like I'm Five." It started as an internet acronym asking for a jargon-free explanation and evolved into a serious communication framework used far outside the internet — in engineering, libraries and even nuclear security.

Why do experts struggle to explain simply?

Because of the curse of knowledge: once you understand something deeply, you forget what it felt like not to understand it. Jargon feels natural to the expert and like a locked door to everyone else. ELI5 is a deliberate workaround for that curse.

How did ELI5 move from slang to a real method?

Between 2016 and 2019 it entered print and professional practice: engineering storytelling in 2016, library genealogy in 2017, and global nuclear security risk verification in 2019. Each case proved the same thing — simplification scales across audiences.

How do I use ELI5 myself?

Explain the idea in plain words, then ask "can you verify?" — let the listener repeat it back in their own experience. For learning, write one "Today I Learned" line a day in five-year-old language. If it is hard to write simply, you have found the exact gap in your understanding.
