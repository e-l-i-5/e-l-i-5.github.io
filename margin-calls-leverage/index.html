Why a 15% Price Dip Erases 100% of Your Money! (Margin Calls Explained)

Crypto & finance, explained like you are five.

How can a tiny 15% market dip completely liquidate your entire trading account? The answer hides behind leverage — the hidden trapdoor of trading — and once you see the math, the door never looks safe again.

Walk through the classic $100 margin trade. You have $20 of real cash; the broker lends you the other $80. You buy $100 of stock with borrowed power. The asset only needs to move 15% against you to trigger disaster — because the losses come out of YOUR $20, not the loan. A 15% drop turns the $100 position into $85; the broker's $80 is still owed; your capital shrinks from $20 to $5. One small market wiggle just slashed 75% of your money.

And the floor beneath you is called the maintenance margin. Brokers demand your equity stay above a minimum line. When a falling price drags you near it, the margin call arrives: a demand to deposit more cash immediately. If you cannot pay, forced liquidation follows — the broker sells your position at the market bottom, and the account is wiped out completely. You did not lose 15%; you lost 100%, on a 15% move.

The real horror is the crowd effect: everyone leveraged is trapped in the same floor at the same time. Forced liquidations trigger cascading sell-offs — accounts dumping stock into a falling market, pushing prices lower, dragging MORE accounts into their own margin calls, forcing MORE selling. The spiral feeds itself.

This exact chain reaction is why the historic Wall Street crash of 1929 became so catastrophic: a market full of borrowed money, a dip, margin calls, liquidations — and a cascade that took the whole system down. Leverage does not just amplify your position; it amplifies everyone's position at once.

The lesson costs nothing to learn in advance: the size of the dip that destroys you depends entirely on how much you borrowed. With 5-to-1 leverage, a small 15% ripple erases every cent — the market does not need a crash to bankrupt a leveraged trader. It only needs a wobble.

The short version

  • A $100 margin trade on $20 real cash ($80 borrowed) means a mere 15% dip cuts your equity from $20 to $5 — a 75% capital loss from one market wiggle.
  • The maintenance margin is the floor: cross it and a margin call demands more cash; without it, forced liquidation sells you out at the bottom — a complete 100% wipeout on a 15% move.
  • Liquidations can cascade: forced selling pushes prices down, triggering more margin calls — the spiral that helped turn the 1929 dip into the Wall Street crash.

FAQ

How can 15% wipe out 100% of a trading account?

Through leverage: with $20 of your own cash and $80 borrowed, a 15% drop in the $100 position leaves $85 — the broker's $80 is owed first, so your $20 equity falls to $5. Past the maintenance margin, forced liquidation sells everything and takes the remaining $5.

What is a margin call?

The broker's demand for extra cash when your equity falls near the maintenance margin — the minimum line they require. Ignore it or fail to fund it, and the broker liquidates your position to protect the loan.

What are cascading sell-offs?

When many leveraged traders get liquidated at once, their forced selling pushes prices even lower — triggering fresh margin calls and more forced selling. The spiral feeds itself until the market crashes.

What does the 1929 crash have to do with margin calls?

The 1929 Wall Street crash ran on exactly this chain reaction: a market crowded with borrowed positions, a price dip, mass margin calls, cascading liquidations — turning an ordinary downturn into a historic collapse.
