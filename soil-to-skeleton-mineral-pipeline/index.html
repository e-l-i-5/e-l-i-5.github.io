The Earth Inside You: How Dirt Turns Into Your Body (Minerals Explained)

Nutrition & biochemistry, explained like you are five.

Ask yourself a strange question: what are you actually made of, down to the tiniest bits? Part of the answer is surprising -- a special part of you comes straight from the ground. Picture your body as a giant LEGO masterpiece. Most of it is built from regular bricks, but the cool spinning wheels and the windows need special pieces. In the body's recipe, those special pieces are minerals -- and here is the twist: they are only about 4% of the recipe. The smallest ingredient on the list is doing some of the most magical work.

Every mineral in you has traveled a road, and the road starts in the dirt. A plant's roots pull minerals up from the soil, the plant ends up on your dinner plate, and the mineral rides along -- from the ground, to the roots, to the plate, and finally into your bones and your blood. You never manufactured a single one. Every special piece in your body was collected, second-hand, from the Earth itself.

Minerals come in two size classes, and the difference is easy to remember. The major minerals are the big bricks -- calcium is the headliner. Your body knows exactly where the big bricks belong: about 99% of your calcium is parked in your bones. Your skeleton is not just a frame; it is the warehouse where the biggest bricks live. The trace minerals are the tiny tools -- iron is the famous one. You need only a pinch of them, but a pinch is not a nothing: some jobs simply cannot be done without the tiny tool that fits.

And iron's job is heroic. Iron powers the oxygen delivery trucks of your blood -- tiny vehicles hauling fuel to every single cell you own. No trucks, no deliveries; no deliveries, and the whole construction site slows down. That is why the smallest bricks and the tiniest tools matter as much as the walls.

But there is one rule that rules them all: the Goldilocks rule. Not too much, not too little -- the amount has to be just right. More is not better when it comes to minerals; the body wants its recipe followed exactly. So how do you collect all your LEGO pieces? The trick is to eat the rainbow: different colored plants pull different minerals out of the same soil, so a colorful plate is the best mining operation your body could ask for.

You are, quite literally, built from the Earth -- borrowed piece by piece, from dirt to dinner to bones and blood.

The short version

  • Minerals are the special LEGO pieces of your body: only about 4% of the recipe, but the part that makes the spinning wheels and windows work.
  • Major minerals are the big bricks (99% of your calcium lives in your bones), trace minerals are the tiny tools (iron runs the oxygen delivery trucks serving every cell).
  • Minerals travel one road: soil, to plant roots, to your dinner plate, to your bones and blood -- so eat the rainbow and keep every amount Goldilocks-just-right.

FAQ

What is the 4% rule?

Minerals make up only about 4% of your body -- the smallest part of the recipe -- yet that smallest part is the most magical. Most of you is built from ordinary material, but the special functions depend on the minerals, just as a LEGO masterpiece needs special pieces for its spinning wheels and windows.

What is the difference between major and trace minerals?

Major minerals are the big bricks, and calcium is the classic example: about 99% of your body's calcium is stored in your bones. Trace minerals are the tiny tools -- you need only small amounts, but the jobs they do, like iron delivering oxygen, cannot be done without them.

Why is iron called an oxygen delivery truck?

Because iron powers the delivery system that hauls oxygen -- the fuel for every cell -- around your whole body. The trucks are tiny, but they never stop, and every cell in the construction site depends on their route.

What does the Goldilocks rule mean for minerals?

Not too much, not too little -- the amount must be just right. Minerals only work when the recipe is followed exactly, which is why variety is the collection strategy: eat the rainbow, because different colored plants carry different minerals up from the same soil.
