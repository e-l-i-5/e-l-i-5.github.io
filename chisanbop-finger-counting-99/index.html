How to Count to 99 on Just 10 Fingers (Chisanbop Hack)

Regular finger counting wastes most of its hardware. Ten fingers, and the standard method quits at ten — each finger just one more unit, thrown away the moment you run out. Did you know your two hands can calculate all the way up to 99? That is the promise of Chisanbop, the finger abacus: an ingenious Korean calculation method that turns your fingers into a living abacus, using positional place values instead of one-finger-one-number.

The secret is that your hands become two different digits of a two-digit number. Your right hand handles the ones place: each finger is worth 1, and the thumb is worth 5 — the 5-thumb trick lets one hand cover 0 to 9 without any gymnastics. Close two fingers and you have 2; close the thumb plus one finger and you have 6. Your left hand copies the same pattern one decimal place up: each finger is worth 10, the thumb is worth 50, so the left hand sweeps from 0 to 90. Two hands, two places, and suddenly ten fingers span the whole range from 0 to 99.

Watch how it assembles a real number. To build 36, press just 3 left fingers — that is 30 — then add your right thumb plus 1 right finger, which is 5 + 1. The number 36 is now visible on your hands: three tens standing up on the left, six ones on the right. Anyone who knows the code can read it instantly, and you can compute by simply adding pressed fingers.

What makes this more than a party trick is that it teaches place value through your own body. Most people feel numbers as a flat run from 1 to 10, because that is how they counted as children. Chisanbop breaks the flatness: the moment your left hand means tens and your right hand means ones, you are literally holding positional notation in your palms. Numbers stop being a single row and become a structure — exactly the mental model behind the abacus, written arithmetic, and math itself.

So the problem with traditional finger counting was never the fingers; it was the mapping. Give every finger the same value and you cap out at ten. Split your hands into places and let the thumb carry five, and the same ten fingers reach 99 — a living abacus you carry everywhere, always ready, no batteries required.

The short version

  • Chisanbop, a Korean finger-abacus method, uses positional place values so two hands can count from 0 to 99 instead of stopping at 10.
  • The right hand is the ones place (each finger = 1, thumb = 5, range 0–9); the left hand is the tens place (each finger = 10, thumb = 50, range 0–90).
  • Building a number is pressing the right combination: 36 = 3 left fingers (30) + right thumb and 1 finger (6).

FAQ

What is Chisanbop?

Chisanbop is a Korean calculation method — literally a "finger abacus" — that turns your two hands into a living abacus. Instead of counting each finger as one unit, it assigns positional place values to your hands, letting them represent every number from 0 to 99.

Why does regular finger counting stop at 10?

Because the traditional method treats all ten fingers as identical units: one finger, one step. With no way to mark groups of ten, the mapping runs out the moment every finger is used. Chisanbop fixes the mapping, not the fingers.

How do the two hands split the work?

The right hand counts the ones place — each finger is worth 1 and the thumb is worth 5, covering 0 to 9. The left hand counts the tens place — each finger is worth 10 and the thumb is worth 50, covering 0 to 90. Together the two places span 0 to 99.

How do you show the number 36 on your hands?

Press 3 fingers on your left hand for the 3 tens (30), then on your right hand press the thumb plus 1 finger for the 6 ones (5 + 1). The combination of pressed fingers displays 36 instantly, and anyone who knows Chisanbop can read it.
